# Viking 1 Lander (Thomas Mutch Memorial Station): Science, Engineering, and Martian Legacy

## Overview & Landing Coordinates
Viking 1 landed in Chryse Planitia on Mars on July 20, 1976. It was the first spacecraft to return images from the surface of Mars and conduct its planned surface science investigations there.

## Why Does Viking 1 Remain on Mars?
Viking 1 was designed as a stationary surface laboratory. Its mission focused on studying the Martian atmosphere, soil, and possible signs of biological activity in place; it was not designed with a Mars ascent and Earth-return system.

The lander greatly exceeded its original 90-day primary mission and continued operating for more than six Earth years. NASA's Viking mission history describes the lander's final operating period in November 1982. NASA's Viking 1 mission page identifies **November 11, 1982** as the date a faulty command caused the lander's high-gain antenna to lose its Earth-pointing orientation. Other NASA summaries use **November 13, 1982** as the end-of-mission date. These dates describe different mission-status milestones and should not be treated as interchangeable without specifying the source's definition.

The lander was later named the **Thomas A. Mutch Memorial Station** in honor of the leader of the Viking imaging team.

## Power Architecture & Instruments
- **Power:** Two SNAP-19 radioisotope thermoelectric generators (RTGs) supplied electrical power using heat from plutonium-238 decay.
- **Biology Investigation:** Viking carried three biology experiments: Pyrolytic Release, Labeled Release, and Gas Exchange. It also carried a Gas Chromatograph–Mass Spectrometer (GCMS) to analyze soil samples.
- **Surface Sampler:** A motorized sampling arm collected Martian soil and delivered samples to onboard instruments.
- **Meteorology:** Instruments measured atmospheric pressure, temperature, and wind conditions.

## Historic Preservation
Viking 1 remains at its landing site on Mars. Claims about its present physical condition should be treated cautiously because no mission has inspected the lander at close range since its operations ended.
